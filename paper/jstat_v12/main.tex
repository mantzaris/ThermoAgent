\author{Anonymous working manuscript}
\date{19 August 2026}

\begin{abstract}
We define and measure a random-sequential stochastic process whose microscopic
degrees of freedom are choices made by independent large-language-model (LLM)
agents. Agent $i$ owns a categorical belief $b_i\in\{-1,+1\}$, categorical
action $a_i\in\{-1,+1\}$, bounded confidence and commitment, private field,
bounded memory, inbox, outbox, and local workload. At each attempted update the
scheduler exposes only the recorded local state and delivered packets; the LLM,
not the scheduler, selects the next belief, action, signal, commitment, memory
state, and typed tool. Reciprocal and directed delivery matrices share edge
support, weighted in/out degree, opportunity schedules, packet schema, and
inference seeds. We characterize isolated-agent kinetic response, collective
order and fluctuations, projected probability currents, finite-sample-corrected
block time-reversal divergence, and a symmetric-layer reference energy. The
exact heat-bath model from the preceding theory study supplies an equilibrium
and quadratic-onset reference, while a fitted kinetic-Ising process separates
effective binary dynamics from residual LLM behavior. \AbstractResult{} The
LLM process is not assumed Bayesian, and whenever first-order closure fails we
interpret reversal divergence only as coarse-grained path irreversibility, not
exact thermodynamic entropy production.
\end{abstract}

\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} nonequilibrium statistical mechanics;
stochastic thermodynamics; nonreciprocal networks; agent-based systems.

\section{Introduction}

Statistical mechanics turns local stochastic rules into predictions about
collective order, fluctuations, relaxation, and irreversible currents. Glauber
dynamics provides a canonical equilibrium construction for binary variables
\cite{glauber1963}; Schnakenberg network theory and stochastic thermodynamics
extend that language to finite Markov systems with sustained probability
currents \cite{schnakenberg1976,lebowitz1999,seifert2005,seifert2012}. The same
formal ingredients occur in decentralized agent systems: local stochastic
choice, neighborhood coupling, persistence, private fields, and directed
information flow. What is usually missing is an empirical microscopic process
whose states are actually chosen by autonomous agents rather than assigned by
an external spin simulator.

Large language models offer a stringent realization. Their choices are
stochastic and context dependent; their hidden activations, sampling state, and
linguistic history need not close under a small visible state. They can also be
instantiated as genuinely separate agents with private observations and
explicit message delivery. This makes them scientifically interesting and
methodologically dangerous. A binary label can support useful kinetic
observables without being a physical spin, but a pathwise KL divergence cannot
be called exact entropy production unless the observed process has an adequate
stationary Markov representation. Hidden-variable and coarse-graining results
make this distinction essential \cite{kawaguchi2013,roldan2012,skinner2021}.

Recent work maps LLM populations to alignment or conformity models and reports
topology-dependent finite-size collective behavior
\cite{han2026,denobili2026,okawa2026}. Those studies establish a useful
connection to statistical physics but do not isolate degree- and
traffic-matched nonreciprocity while measuring state-space currents. Conversely,
asymmetric kinetic-Ising theory directly relates directed coupling to
irreversibility \cite{aguilera2021,zhang2023,dicarlo2025}, but its binary units
are prescribed stochastic variables rather than text-conditioned agents with
private memory, messages, commitments, and typed local consequences.

Here we connect these levels prospectively. We first fit an individual kinetic
response law from controlled information states. We then run the same pinned
LLM as independent agents on reciprocal and nonreciprocal fixed-degree networks.
The design counterbalances semantic labels, holds communication quantity fixed,
uses paired orientations and exogenous stochastic tapes, and treats a whole
graph trajectory as the independent unit. Our claims concern the measured
choice process, not intelligence, application performance, physical heat, or a
thermodynamic limit.

\section{Related statistical mechanics and agent literature}

Logit response in strategic interaction already admits a statistical-mechanical
interpretation when utilities derive from a potential \cite{blume1993}.
Reciprocal binary couplings similarly admit a Gibbs reference; asymmetric
couplings generically break detailed balance. Modern treatments of asymmetric
kinetic-Ising systems use response, delayed correlations, and entropy production
to characterize the resulting dynamics \cite{aguilera2021}. Nonreciprocal
interactions can also create collective states with no equilibrium analogue
\cite{fruchart2021}; our finite LLM networks are too small to establish such a
phase transition, so we report finite-size collective-regime changes only.

Trajectory reversal provides an observable route to irreversibility.
For a stationary process, the KL divergence between forward and reverse paths
lower-bounds dissipative entropy production when relevant hidden coordinates are
discarded \cite{roldan2012,kawaguchi2013}. Transfer entropy instead measures
lagged predictive information and is not itself entropy production
\cite{schreiber2000}. Both quantities suffer finite-sample bias, particularly
for sparse categorical words; we therefore report pseudocount sensitivity,
time-shuffled floors, information-permutation floors, and graph-level intervals
\cite{paninski2003}.

The closest LLM studies probe conformity, intrinsic field bias, and
temperature-driven crossovers \cite{han2026,denobili2026,okawa2026}. V12 adds
separate belief and executed-action layers, directed typed packets,
degree-preserving orientation pairs, state-current estimators, and an explicit
comparison among exact heat-bath theory, a fitted binary surrogate, and direct
LLM paths. The contribution is not the general fact that asymmetry can create
entropy production; it is the controlled empirical realization and the
measurement boundary imposed by LLM memory and coarse graining.

\section{Decentralized LLM-agent microdynamics}

\subsection{Agent state and information boundary}

For agent $i$ at attempted update $t$, the recorded local state is
\begin{equation}
 x_i(t)=\bigl(b_i(t),a_i(t),c_i(t),q_i(t),m_i(t),w_i(t)\bigr),
 \label{eq:localstate}
\end{equation}
where $b_i,a_i\in\{-1,+1\}$ are decoded categorical belief and action,
$c_i\in[0,1]$ is self-reported confidence, $q_i$ is one of five bounded
commitment states, $m_i$ is one of five explicit memory states, and $w_i$ is a
bounded local workload. Each agent additionally owns a fixed private field
$h_i\in\{-1,0,+1\}$, a role, inbox, outbox, and---only in the secondary
regime---a three-entry private memory history. The evaluator records
$X_t=(x_1,\ldots,x_N)$ and message state, but no agent receives $X_t$.

\begin{figure}[t]
 \centering
 \includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{\figroot/figure_01_agent_architecture.pdf}
 \caption{Independent-agent information and authority boundary. A scheduler
 offers a random-sequential update and transports a validated model-selected
 packet; it never selects the belief, action, signal, commitment, memory state,
 or tool. Global state is evaluator-only.}
 \label{fig:architecture}
\end{figure}

The LLM returns exactly one schema-validated object containing categorical
belief and action, confidence, commitment, bounded memory state, outgoing
signal, typed tool, and reason code. A failed response receives one deterministic
format repair. If that also fails, the attempted update leaves the scientific
state unchanged; no centralized replacement is inserted. Executing the selected
tool changes only the scheduled agent's bounded workload and can improve or
worsen it depending on the relation between the LLM-selected action and private
field. This creates a consequential local action without turning an application
simulator into the primary contribution.

\subsection{Markovized and persistent-memory regimes}

The primary prompt is rebuilt at each attempt from the recorded current local
state, delivered inbox, private field, role, protocol text, and current clock.
No chat history is retained. This is a Markovization intervention, not proof of
first-order closure: model sampling and omitted internal variables may still
leave detectable history dependence. The secondary persistent-memory regime
adds only the agent's own last three bounded state summaries. We compare
history-order likelihoods and conditional mutual information in both regimes.

The pinned model is Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct \cite{qwen2024}, revision
\texttt{\seqsplit{a09a35458c702b33eeacc393d103063234e8bc28}}, loaded with NF4
double quantization and BF16 computation. Sampling temperature and top-$p$ are
generation controls. We do not identify decoding temperature with physical or
effective thermodynamic temperature.

\subsection{Latent-label neutralization}

The preceding qualification study found a strong displayed-right preference.
V12 treats this as an external-field artifact. Amber and cobalt are described
symmetrically; which label decodes to latent $+1$ is counterbalanced, display
order is randomized, private fields and disordered initial states are balanced,
and the same mapping and random tape are used within matched network arms. Only
decoded spins enter statistical analysis.

\section{Reciprocal and nonreciprocal delivery networks}

Let $A$ be a symmetric fixed-degree support and $W_s$ its symmetric
row-stochastic weight matrix. A cycle-basis construction gives an antisymmetric,
divergence-free circulation $A_a$, with $A_a^\top=-A_a$ and
$A_a\bm 1=0$. Delivery weights are
\begin{equation}
 W(\alpha)=W_s+\alpha A_a,\qquad
 \alpha\in\{0,0.20,0.50,0.80\}.
 \label{eq:weights}
\end{equation}
The circulation is normalized so every weight remains nonnegative at the
largest $\alpha$. Thus support, total weight, every weighted in/out degree, one
message opportunity per valid update, packet schema, and payload size remain
fixed. $W(\alpha)^\top$ supplies the paired reverse orientation.

\begin{figure}[t]
 \centering
 \includegraphics[width=0.92\textwidth]{\figroot/figure_02_network_construction.pdf}
 \caption{Reciprocal and directed matrices on common support. Directionality
 changes recipient probabilities without changing weighted degree or message
 opportunities.}
 \label{fig:network}
\end{figure}

We use a degree-two ring and a degree-three modular graph. Keeping degree fixed
is important: the earlier V10 modular construction increased degree with $N$,
saturated an unnormalized local field, and produced apparent large-$N$ freezing.
That historical anomaly is not used as V12 evidence.

At each attempted update, the precomputed tape selects one agent, display-order
paraphrase, sampling seed, and recipient uniform variate. The LLM independently
selects packet content. The delivery matrix maps the common recipient variate to
an authorized neighbor. One sweep is $N$ attempted updates.

\section{Equilibrium-reference observable and empirical response models}

Using the symmetric influence layer, we record the effective reference energy
\begin{equation}
 H_{\rm ref}(X)=
 -\frac{J_b}{2}\bm b^\top W_s\bm b
 -\frac{J_a}{2}\bm a^\top D_s\bm a
 -K\bm a^\top\bm b-\bm h^\top\bm b-\bm g^\top\bm a.
 \label{eq:href}
\end{equation}
Here $D_s=W_s$, $(J_b,J_a,K)=(1,0.65,0.8)$ define a dimensionless diagnostic,
and $\bm g=0$ in the primary network. This is not a Hamiltonian claimed to
generate Qwen's stationary distribution. In the V10 heat-bath reference the
same form does define a Gibbs process under reciprocal coupling; its exact
detailed-balance and quadratic-onset results are retained solely as a mechanistic
reference.

Isolated-agent calls span private field, delivered neighbor field, current
belief/action, coupling relevance, decoding-noise control, and memory regime.
We fit held-out logistic kinetic, nonlinear additive, and persistence-interaction
models for both belief and action. A separate fitted kinetic-Ising surrogate is
then simulated under the V12 graphs. It receives no LLM trajectory outcome and
is labeled an effective comparator rather than LLM evidence.

\section{Statistical-mechanics observables}

The belief and action order parameters and cross-layer overlap are
\begin{equation}
 m_b=N^{-1}\sum_i b_i,\qquad m_a=N^{-1}\sum_i a_i,
 \qquad q_{ba}=N^{-1}\sum_i b_i a_i.
\end{equation}
We record edge disagreement density, $N\operatorname{Var}(m_b)$ and
$N\operatorname{Var}(m_a)$ without temperature normalization, graph-distance
correlations, integrated autocorrelation time, and relaxation from ordered and
disordered starts. A predeclared field sweep supplies finite-system hysteresis
area; no thermodynamic-limit hysteresis is inferred.

For $N\leq4$, the primary categorical projection is the exact integer encoding
of $(\bm b,\bm a)$. For larger $N$, a fixed coarse macrostate bins
$(m_b,m_a,q_{ba},H_{\rm ref}/N)$. We estimate Shannon configuration and
macrostate entropy with Miller--Madow sensitivity, and report Tsallis
$q\in\{0.5,2\}$ and Gini--Simpson diversity only as predeclared secondary
summaries. There is no search over $q$.

Neighbor mutual information and directed transfer entropy use permutation
floors. The latter is a predictive dependence statistic, not a thermodynamic
rate. Layer-projected belief and action irreversibility are likewise diagnostic
projections and are not assumed additive.

\section{Currents, entropy production, and path reversal}

For an adequately sampled first-order categorical path, transition counts give
a regularized kernel $P_{xy}$ and stationary vector $\pi$. The projected current
and Markov entropy-production statistic are
\begin{align}
 J_{xy}&=\pi_xP_{xy}-\pi_yP_{yx},\\
 \widehat\sigma_{\rm M}
 &=\frac12\sum_{x,y}J_{xy}
 \log\frac{\pi_xP_{xy}}{\pi_yP_{yx}}.
 \label{eq:epr}
\end{align}
We report nats per attempted update and $N\widehat\sigma_{\rm M}$ nats per
sweep, with pseudocounts $0.1,0.5,1.0$. Because sparse smoothing can itself
produce a positive estimate, reciprocal values and shuffled controls are
essential.

Our primary scalable irreversibility statistic is block reversal divergence.
For a word length $L$,
\begin{equation}
 \widehat{\mathcal I}_L=\frac{1}{L-1}
 \sum_{\omega}\widehat p(\omega)
 \log\frac{\widehat p(\omega)}{\widehat p(\omega^R)}.
 \label{eq:block}
\end{equation}
The primary $L=3$ value is corrected by the mean of 200 within-panel
time-shuffled paths. We also examine $L=2,4$, coarse-graining widths, burn-in,
and trajectory truncation. Excess irreversibility subtracts the matched
reciprocal panel,
\begin{equation}
 \Delta\mathcal I_L(\alpha)=\mathcal I_L(\alpha)-\mathcal I_L(0).
\end{equation}
When Markov closure is inadequate, Eq.~\eqref{eq:block} is described only as
coarse-grained pathwise irreversibility or a lower bound on hidden dissipation.

\section{Prospective experimental design}

An engineering pilot could inspect only schema validity, latent occupancy,
bidirectional transition counts, delivery, privacy, and runtime. It used only
reciprocal graphs and never computed currents, reversal divergence, or a
nonreciprocity effect. The final attempt had balanced occupancy and passed all
estimability criteria. The formal protocol was then frozen at SHA-256
\texttt{\seqsplit{cee49ca7111b81ae9544a8d32a754ce4c9f15bff16333e98588147a7f7f665b6}}
with execution source
\texttt{\seqsplit{0796286362ec4dde0eb4f2dc88ecea4c3bf53859e618aec1928ba8b9e8b0a154}}.

The isolated response block contains 576 decisions. Small systems use
$N\in\{3,4\}$, four graph/environment replications, 60 sweeps, and nine-sweep
burn-in. Collective systems use $N\in\{8,16\}$, ring and modular graphs, a
$2\times2$ grid of neighbor relevance $(0.35,0.80)$ and decoding-noise control
$(0.50,0.85)$, two replications, eight sweeps, and two-sweep burn-in. Exact
matched blocks cover bounded memory, relaxation, six communication controls,
and a bidirectional field sweep. Every reciprocal arm is paired with forward
and transposed orientations at positive $\alpha$.

Graph trajectory is the independent unit. Primary intervals use 10,000 cluster
bootstrap resamples; secondary families use Holm correction. Quadratic,
linear, and linear-plus-quadratic weak-$\alpha$ models are compared by
leave-cluster-out prediction and descriptive AICc. No result-dependent stop or
parameter change is allowed after freeze.

The first post-formal analysis invocation stopped before writing result tables
because a replicate identifier present in the frozen design was not copied into
the derived panel-summary row used by the factor analysis. A separately logged
bookkeeping wrapper copied that existing identifier and reran the unchanged
estimands. No trajectory, grouping rule, contrast, seed, threshold, or
exclusion changed; the failed invocation and repair checksum are retained.

\section{Discussion}

V12 separates three evidentiary levels. The heat-bath reference has an exact
finite-state Gibbs equilibrium in its reciprocal limit and an exact
finite-kernel entropy-production rate. The fitted kinetic-Ising process is a
numerical effective model learned from isolated categorical responses. The LLM
results are empirical trajectories generated by independent contexts and local
messages. Agreement among their curve shapes is mechanistic evidence; it does
not make their numerical rates identical.

The design also separates autonomy from anthropomorphism. Each LLM call owns and
changes a persistent local state, chooses a consequential local tool, and emits
content delivered only through authorized edges. No global optimizer chooses
the responses. Nevertheless, these are synthetic binary coordination choices
from one instruction model, not human organizations or field-deployed agents.

Potential nonzero reciprocal irreversibility has several interpretations:
finite-sample bias, option or prompt asymmetry, cyclic scan structure, hidden
model state, irreversible commitment/workload coordinates, or a genuinely
non-potential local response. Subtracting the paired reciprocal arm isolates an
excess associated with directed delivery but does not identify literal heat.
Message permutation and placebo controls ask whether the excess depends on
informative directed content rather than prompt volume alone.

The principal empirical result is a boundary rather than the proposed positive
nonreciprocity effect. The LLM's microscopic transition law was measurably
neighbor-sensitive, and ordinary coupling and decoding-noise controls shifted
collective order, fluctuations, and relaxation. Degree- and traffic-matched
directionality nevertheless produced neither a replicated excess reversal
divergence nor the V10 heat-bath model's quadratic onset. This separates the
existence of local social response from the stronger claim that an asymmetric
delivery graph creates an observable irreversible current on the sampled time
and state scales.

The same construction can be interpreted as private reports passed among
humanitarian coordinators or one-way telemetry among defensive utility agents.
Those mappings change the semantics of fields, commitments, and messages but
not the present estimand. We did not simulate service delivery, infrastructure
restoration, or human decisions in V12 and make no application-performance
claim.

\section{Limitations}

First, the primary model is one quantized Qwen revision. Inference seeds and
graph realizations support replication within that implementation, not model
universality. Second, the large-network paths are short because every attempted
update is a GPU generation. Their collective summaries are finite-time and
finite-size. Third, categorical coarse graining hides token-level and activation
dynamics. Block reversal divergence is therefore a lower-bound-style observable
when closure fails. Fourth, the scheduler's update opportunity and a recipient
uniform are exogenous; the primary experiment intentionally fixes opportunity
volume to identify topology. Fifth, the typed workload is a bounded abstract
consequence. Humanitarian coordination and defensive utility restoration are
interpretive mappings only; no application benefit is tested.

\section{Conclusion}

We provide a reproducible stochastic-mechanical construction in which actual
decentralized LLM agents, rather than a central spin simulator, generate the
microscopic transitions. Fixed-degree reciprocal/directed pairs permit a clean
test of information-flow asymmetry; local response models connect language
decisions to kinetic binary dynamics; current and reversal estimators state
their normalization and bias floor. The resulting claim is deliberately
conditional on the observed data and Markov diagnostics. Negative, mixed, and
positive hypotheses are retained alike.

For this pinned model, the supported claim is that independent LLM choices form
a coupled, fluctuating finite network whose order and relaxation respond to
neighbor information and decision noise. The stronger claim that controlled
nonreciprocity generates measurable excess irreversibility is not supported at
the sampled sizes and trajectory lengths. That distinction is the main
statistical-mechanical result.

\section{Structured transition and wire packet}

Every valid packet contains schema version, sender, time, belief spin, action
spin, outgoing signal, quantized confidence, commitment code, memory code, and
CRC framing. Primary arms use exactly the same binary serializer. Attempted,

\section{Estimator sensitivity}

The compact tables retain raw plug-in Markov statistics, three pseudocounts,

\section{Prospective hypotheses}

H1 tests whether delivered neighbor state changes the local LLM transition law
after counterbalancing. H2 tests reciprocal versus directed current or path
irreversibility at matched traffic. H3 tests a dose response to quantified
nonreciprocity. H4 compares a positive quadratic weak-$\alpha$ component with
linear alternatives and V10. H5 tests collective order/fluctuation response to
coupling, noise, or directionality. H6 requires directional replication across
paired orientations and two sizes. Scientific completion does not require all
hypotheses to be positive.
