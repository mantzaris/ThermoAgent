\begin{abstract}
We formulate a decentralized autonomous-agent system as a coupled belief--action
process on a multiplex network. Each agent has private state, persistent memory,
an inbox and outbox, and a local stochastic policy; a scheduler supplies update
opportunities but never chooses collective actions. In a restricted static,
symmetric, memoryless limit, the local heat-bath rule obeys detailed balance
with respect to an explicit two-layer Hamiltonian. Exact enumeration validates
the Gibbs stationary distribution and the nonequilibrium free-energy identity.
Directed communication, private-field disorder, memory, task injection, and
topology changes break this equilibrium structure. We quantify those departures
using probability currents and entropy production, and numerically examine order
parameters, Binder cumulants, response peaks, relaxation, hysteresis, topology,
and finite-size effects. The numerical evidence is described as a
\ResultTransitionLanguage, not assumed to be a thermodynamic-limit phase
transition. Generalized Tsallis entropies are evaluated prospectively but
\ResultTsallisSummary. Humanitarian logistics and defensive cyber-utility
restoration are included only as interpretive mappings of the same microdynamics.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Autonomous multi-agent systems are often described in the language of
coordination, consensus, congestion, and cascades. Statistical mechanics offers
a more disciplined question: can one specify a microstate and stochastic local
dynamics for such a system, identify an equilibrium reference measure, and then
measure how operational features drive it away from that reference? This is
different from attaching an ``energy'' label to an arbitrary performance score.

Kinetic Ising dynamics supplies a canonical local stochastic update whose
reversible limit is analytically controlled \cite{glauber1963}. Logit revision
also has a direct interpretation in local strategic interaction
\cite{blume1993}. Network substrates change collective critical behavior
\cite{dorogovtsev2008}, and multiplex networks provide a natural representation
when communication and operational dependence are not the same graph
\cite{kivela2014}. These ideas have been used broadly in agent-based and social
dynamics \cite{castellano2009}, but a distinction is often lost between agents
as independent decision-making units and spins as centrally updated labels.

We make that distinction executable. An agent owns its belief, action, memory,
workload, private fields, commitments, inbox, and outbox. It samples a local
policy from authorized information. The simulator schedules updates and applies
transitions, but receives no authority to replace the proposal by a centralized
choice. The primary model is deliberately minimal so its equilibrium limit can
be proved. Application mappings are secondary.

The contributions are: (i) an explicit coupled belief--action Hamiltonian and
detailed-balance proof; (ii) an exact finite-state validation including the
Gibbs law and a relative-entropy free-energy identity; (iii) a controlled route
to nonequilibrium dynamics and exact stationary entropy production under
directed coupling; (iv) numerical finite-size, topology, disorder, relaxation,
and hysteresis analyses; and (v) a precise account of where Shannon entropy is
or is not supplemented by generalized entropy.

\section{Autonomous-agent microdynamics}

\subsection{Microstate and multiplex structure}

There are $N$ persistent agents. Agent $i$ has a binary belief or intention
$b_i\in\{-1,+1\}$, an executed or committed action
$a_i\in\{-1,+1\}$, a slow memory $m_i$, and a workload $w_i$. The full
equilibrium microstate is
\begin{equation}
  x=(\bm b,\bm a)\in\{-1,+1\}^{2N}.
\end{equation}
Memory and workload are held fixed at zero in the equilibrium reference and are
activated only in driven extensions. Communication and operational dependence
are represented by adjacency matrices $A^{(c)}$ and $A^{(d)}$, respectively.
They can differ in topology and, outside equilibrium, in direction and time.

Figure~\ref{fig:architecture} shows the executable information boundary. A
policy call receives local private state and explicitly delivered messages,
never the global microstate or another private vault.

\subsection{Equilibrium-reference Hamiltonian}

All quantities below are nondimensional. We define
\begin{align}
 H(x)={}&-\frac{J_b}{2}\sum_{ij}A^{(c)}_{ij}b_i b_j
         -\frac{J_a}{2}\sum_{ij}A^{(d)}_{ij}a_i a_j
         -K\sum_i a_i b_i \nonumber\\
       &-\sum_i h_i b_i-\sum_i g_i a_i .
 \label{eq:hamiltonian}
\end{align}
Here $J_b$ controls belief alignment, $J_a$ action compatibility, $K$ local
belief--action consistency, $h_i$ private evidence, and $g_i$ local task drive.
This is a genuine Hamiltonian only when both layers are symmetric and static,
all agents share a temperature, and memory, delay, loss, and task injection are
disabled. Elsewhere we call its symmetric part an energy diagnostic, not an
equilibrium energy.

\subsection{Local heat-bath decisions}

One agent-variable is selected uniformly at each asynchronous update. Conditional
on the rest of the microstate, the local fields are
\begin{align}
 \ell_i^{(b)}&=J_b\sum_j A^{(c)}_{ij}b_j+K a_i+h_i,\\
 \ell_i^{(a)}&=J_a\sum_j A^{(d)}_{ij}a_j+K b_i+g_i.
\end{align}
The selected variable $s_i$ is set to $+1$ with
\begin{equation}
 \Pr(s_i=+1\mid\mathcal F_i)
 =\left[1+\exp\left(-2\ell_i/T\right)\right]^{-1}.
 \label{eq:heatbath}
\end{equation}
The decision temperature $T>0$ is therefore the stochasticity of a local
softmax policy. It is specified before simulation and is not fitted from
performance.

For large equilibrium runs we use chromatic block Gibbs sampling. Nodes of a
color class are an independent set, so their conditional updates factorize.
Each block kernel preserves the same Gibbs measure; color order is randomized
between sweeps. The exact detailed-balance calculation uses the asynchronous
single-variable kernel, not the block implementation.

\section{Equilibrium reference}

\subsection{Detailed balance and stationary distribution}

Suppose $A^{(c)}$ and $A^{(d)}$ are symmetric and static, $m_i=w_i=0$, and
agents share $T$. Let $x$ and $y$ differ in exactly one variable. From
equation~\eqref{eq:heatbath},
\begin{equation}
 \frac{W_{xy}}{W_{yx}}
 =\exp\left[-\frac{H(y)-H(x)}{T}\right].
 \label{eq:localbalance}
\end{equation}
For
\begin{equation}
 \pi(x)=Z^{-1}\exp[-H(x)/T],\qquad
 Z=\sum_x\exp[-H(x)/T],
 \label{eq:gibbs}
\end{equation}
equation~\eqref{eq:localbalance} implies
$\pi(x)W_{xy}=\pi(y)W_{yx}$. Self transitions are trivially balanced, and
non-neighboring state pairs have zero transition probability in both directions.
Thus the finite irreducible heat-bath chain is reversible and equation
\eqref{eq:gibbs} is its unique stationary distribution.

Exact enumeration at $N=3$ gives a maximum detailed-balance flux residual of
\ResultDetailedBalanceResidual. The maximum empirical total-variation distance
from the Gibbs law over the frozen temperature grid is \ResultTVMax. A
representative comparison appears in figure~\ref{fig:gibbs}.

\subsection{Nonequilibrium free-energy identity}

For any distribution $p$ over equilibrium microstates, define
\begin{equation}
 F[p]=\sum_xp(x)H(x)+T\sum_xp(x)\log p(x).
\end{equation}
Substitution of $\log\pi(x)=-H(x)/T-\log Z$ gives the exact identity
\begin{equation}
 F[p]-F[\pi]=T D_{\mathrm{KL}}(p\Vert\pi)\ge 0.
 \label{eq:freeenergykl}
\end{equation}
The largest numerical closure residual in the exact suite is
\ResultFreeEnergyResidual. This identity requires the explicit Hamiltonian and
reference law; it is not asserted for the directed or driven models.

\section{Mean-field approximation and stability}

For homogeneous mean degrees $z_c,z_d$ and uniform fields, the mean-field order
parameters satisfy
\begin{align}
 m_b&=\tanh[(J_bz_cm_b+Km_a+h)/T],\\
 m_a&=\tanh[(J_az_dm_a+Km_b+g)/T].
 \label{eq:meanfield}
\end{align}
At zero field, linearization about $(m_b,m_a)=(0,0)$ gives
\begin{equation}
 \begin{pmatrix}m_b\\m_a\end{pmatrix}_{t+1}
 \simeq \frac{1}{T}
 \begin{pmatrix}J_bz_c&K\\K&J_az_d\end{pmatrix}
 \begin{pmatrix}m_b\\m_a\end{pmatrix}_{t}.
\end{equation}
The disordered state loses stability when the largest eigenvalue equals $T$.
For quenched independent communication dilution $p_c$, the first approximation
is $z_c\mapsto p_cz_c$. Correlations, finite components, topology, and disorder
make this a boundary prediction rather than an exact transition temperature.

\section{Nonequilibrium dynamics and entropy production}

Private heterogeneous fields preserve a Gibbs description when static, but
directed interactions need not derive from equation~\eqref{eq:hamiltonian}.
Delays, stale messages, loss, partitions, persistent memory, workload injection,
and time-dependent fields further break the reversible reference.

For a finite stationary Markov chain with stationary probabilities $P_x$ and
kernel $W_{xy}$, define currents $J_{xy}=P_xW_{xy}-P_yW_{yx}$. The exact total
entropy-production rate per update is \cite{schnakenberg1976,seifert2012}
\begin{equation}
 \dot S_{\mathrm{tot}}=
 \frac{1}{2}\sum_{x,y}J_{xy}
 \log\frac{P_xW_{xy}}{P_yW_{yx}}\ge0.
 \label{eq:epr}
\end{equation}
It is zero in the reversible suite to within
\ResultEquilibriumEPR. Breaking communication reciprocity produces the response
in figure~\ref{fig:epr}; the high-asymmetry mean is \ResultDrivenEPR.

\begin{figure}[t]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=0.68\textwidth]{figure_06_entropy_production.pdf}
  \caption{Exact stationary entropy production after directed communication
  breaks detailed balance. Intervals are across independent edge-orientation
  realizations, not time steps.}
  \label{fig:epr}
\end{figure}

For time-dependent application protocols we log work from field changes,
$\delta W=-\sum_i\delta h_i b_i-\sum_i\delta g_i a_i$, and transition
affinities $\log(W_{xy}/W_{yx})$. We do not claim a Crooks relation or split
housekeeping and excess terms because the application-level coarse state is not
shown to satisfy the required trajectory assumptions
\cite{crooks1999,esposito2010}.

\section{Observables and numerical method}

The primary order parameters are belief order $m_b=N^{-1}\sum_i b_i$, action
order $m_a$, combined order $m=(m_b+m_a)/2$, and belief--action consistency
$q_{ba}=N^{-1}\sum_i a_ib_i$. Response quantities are
\begin{align}
 \chi &= N\operatorname{Var}(m)/T,\\
 C &= \operatorname{Var}(H)/(NT^2),\\
 U_4 &= 1-\frac{\langle m^4\rangle}{3\langle m^2\rangle^2}.
\end{align}
We also record activity, mutual information between belief and action,
integrated autocorrelation time, effective sample size, and relaxation from an
ordered state. Binder intersections and response peaks follow standard
finite-size logic \cite{binder1981}; on network substrates, topology-dependent
critical behavior is expected \cite{dorogovtsev2008}.

The frozen protocol contains five lattice sizes, six independent realizations
per finite-size cell, four topology families, communication dilution, private
field disorder, and fresh seeds. Burn-in and thinning were selected from a
retained pilot before formal execution. Correlated Monte Carlo samples are not
treated as inferential replicates.

Configurational Shannon entropy is computed from the empirical coarse
magnetization distribution. The secondary family
\begin{equation}
 S_q(p)=\frac{1-\sum_kp_k^q}{q-1}
\end{equation}
uses a prespecified small set of $q$, including the Shannon limit and the
$q=2$ Gini--Simpson form \cite{tsallis1988}. This comparison is diagnostic:
no $q$ is selected post hoc. Equal trajectory lengths make the estimators
comparable within the frozen grid, but no separate finite-sample bias correction
is claimed; that omission limits interpretation of small entropy differences.

\section{Application mappings}

The abstract model is mapped, without parameter retuning, to humanitarian
multi-commodity coordination and defensive cyber-utility restoration. The
humanitarian drive injects correlated workload, constrains shared service
capacity, and allows unresolved shortage to propagate on the dependency layer.
The utility drive removes service, corrupts selected local fields, makes
communication asymmetric, imposes a temporary partition, constrains crews, and
allows dependency-mediated secondary failures. No offensive cyber procedure or
real infrastructure target is represented.

Figure~\ref{fig:networks} shows one prespecified snapshot from each mapping.
Figure~\ref{fig:utility} shows the utility ensemble trajectory. Workload and
resources are reconstructed from separate injected, cascaded, completed, and

\section{Discussion}

The principal conceptual result is a boundary between a literal mathematical
construction and an analogy. Equation~\eqref{eq:hamiltonian}, its Gibbs law,
detailed balance, and equation~\eqref{eq:freeenergykl} are exact in the stated
limit. Decision temperature is a policy-noise parameter. Once communication is
directed or delayed, memory and workload evolve, or the environment is driven,
the equilibrium Hamiltonian no longer governs the full process. Entropy
production then follows from transition currents, not from an operational
severity score.

The numerical results support \ResultOverallClaim. They do not prove a
thermodynamic-limit singularity, universal critical exponents, literal physical
heat, or field validity of either application mapping. The generalized-entropy
comparison gives \ResultTsallisDetail; consequently Shannon entropy remains the
default unless a future model establishes nonextensive structure independently.

Autonomy matters to the interpretation. Each local update belongs to a
persistent agent and can change under a private-observation counterfactual
without mutating peer state. Nevertheless, the binary model is not a language
model, a trained reinforcement-learning policy, or a high-fidelity logistics or
power-system solver. Its value is analytical transparency.

\section{Conclusion}

We have given a decentralized belief--action system an explicit microstate,
Hamiltonian, reversible local policy, Gibbs reference law, and controlled
nonequilibrium extensions. Exact enumeration verifies the equilibrium
identities; directed interaction yields positive probability-current entropy
production; and finite systems exhibit a topology-, communication-, and
disorder-dependent coordination crossover with response and relaxation
signatures. The result is a statistical-mechanics formulation of agentic
coordination, not evidence that entropy is a superior controller.

\section{Exact state enumeration}

For $N$ agents the binary state space has $2^{2N}$ configurations. We enumerate
all states, calculate $H(x)$, normalize equation~\eqref{eq:gibbs} with a shifted
log weight, and construct the full asynchronous heat-bath kernel. The stationary
distribution of a directed kernel is obtained by solving
$P^\top\bm\pi=\bm\pi$ with normalization. All matrix rows and probability
vectors are checked for finite values and unit mass.
